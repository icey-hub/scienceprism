\title{From Detection Performance to Explanation Fidelity: An Exploratory Evaluation on the GADBench Reddit Graph}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Graph neural networks (GNNs) use node attributes and neighborhood structure for anomaly detection. However, predictive performance does not establish explanation fidelity or causal validity. We used the SciencePrism controlled experiment runner to compare a two-layer graph convolutional network (GCN) with feature-only logistic regression on the GADBench Reddit graph. We also conducted an edge-mask deletion pilot on a fixed, small sample. Across three official splits of the same graph, mean AUPRC was $0.0620\pm0.0064$ for the GCN and $0.0605\pm0.0047$ for logistic regression. Mean AUROC was $0.6695\pm0.0197$ and $0.6594\pm0.0087$, respectively. These differences are descriptive because the splits share one graph. In the explanation pilot, 9 of 12 stratified test nodes from split 0 yielded explanations. The median difference in predicted-class logit drop between mask-ranked edges and 20 equal-size random deletions was $0.0025$. The mean was $0.4585$, driven by three values near $1.377$ from nodes labeled and predicted as normal; the mean for anomaly-labeled cases was $-0.0015$. These results indicate instance-level heterogeneity and do not establish explanation reliability. We distinguish detection labels, model-prediction perturbations, and explanation ground truth, and discuss limits arising from a single graph, transductive evaluation, a small explanation sample, and deletion-induced distribution shift.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} graph anomaly detection; graph neural networks; local explanations; fidelity; GADBench; GNNExplainer

\section{Introduction}
Node anomaly detection combines a difficult ranking problem with an application-dependent definition of what counts as anomalous. An anomalous node may be unusual in its attributes, its structural position, or the agreement between those two views. Labels are also scarce in many settings, so methods trained from labeled anomalies and methods that infer outliers from reconstruction error address different information regimes. For example, DOMINANT learns node representations with a graph convolutional encoder and scores nodes using attribute and structure reconstruction errors\cite{ding2019dominant}. By contrast, GADBench defines a supervised static-graph benchmark and compares 29 models over ten real-world datasets\cite{tang2023gadbench}. It reports that tree ensembles with simple neighborhood aggregation can outperform GNNs tailored to graph anomaly detection. The benchmark therefore motivates direct comparisons rather than an assumption that message passing will help.

Detection performance is only one part of an explainable anomaly-detection claim. A post-hoc explainer usually describes why a fitted model produced a particular output; that output may or may not agree with the dataset label, and neither agreement nor an importance score identifies the real-world cause of an anomaly. GNNExplainer optimizes compact edge and feature masks for a particular prediction\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer}, while PGExplainer learns a parameterized explanation generator that can be applied across instances\cite{luo2020pgexplainer}. These methods make clear that a mask is an object of analysis in its own right. Its quality depends on which prediction is explained, how the mask is evaluated, and whether the perturbation used for evaluation is plausible. Systematic studies consequently distinguish fidelity dimensions and show that rankings can change with the evaluation protocol\cite{amara2022graphframex,agarwal2022probing,zheng2024robust}.

Here we use the SciencePrism controlled experiment runner to conduct a bounded evaluation on the GADBench Reddit graph. The first question is whether a two-layer graph convolutional network (GCN) improves anomaly ranking over a feature-only logistic-regression baseline under the same official data masks. The second is whether, for a prespecified small sample of test nodes, deleting the top-ranked edges from a local edge mask changes the model's predicted-class logit more than deleting an equal number of random candidate edges. The first question concerns detection performance; the second concerns local sensitivity of a fitted GCN. They are related but not interchangeable.

This study contributes an auditable, single-graph case study rather than a new detector or explainer. It records source-data hashes and uses the benchmark's three official masks, reports the validation-based model-selection outcomes alongside test metrics, and exposes the node-level explanation perturbations instead of relying on one aggregate. The explanation pilot is deliberately interpreted as exploratory: a positive deletion difference is evidence about the model's response to that particular perturbation, not evidence that the selected edges are the cause of anomalous behavior.

Dataset labels provide node-level ground truth for detection only; no edge-level explanation ground truth was available. All conclusions are therefore restricted to this graph, these model implementations, and the recorded perturbation protocol.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Graph Anomaly Detection}
Graph anomaly detectors differ in the supervision available and in how they define abnormality. Reconstruction-based approaches can operate without node labels: DOMINANT, for example, combines graph convolution with a deep autoencoder and uses attribute- and structure-reconstruction errors to score nodes\cite{ding2019dominant}. Supervised approaches instead learn to separate labeled anomalous and normal nodes, which makes their results dependent on the label definition and split protocol. GADBench provides a broad evaluation of this supervised setting, comparing 29 models on ten real-world datasets and finding that simple tree ensembles with neighborhood aggregation can be highly competitive\cite{tang2023gadbench}. These families answer different questions, so this paper does not treat a supervised GCN as a proxy for graph anomaly detection as a whole.

The present comparison uses a two-layer GCN, which aggregates normalized neighborhood information together with node attributes\cite{kipf2017gcn}, and a feature-only logistic-regression baseline. The latter provides a transparent check on whether the tested GCN improves on a linear decision rule over the same node attributes. It is intentionally narrower than the tree-ensemble and neighborhood-aggregation baselines emphasized by GADBench, and the experiment does not re-run its full benchmark. Accordingly, the results should be read as a within-dataset comparison of two specified models rather than a new ranking of anomaly-detection methods.

\subsection{Local GNN Explanations and Fidelity}
GNNExplainer formulates explanation as an optimization problem and identifies compact subgraphs and feature subsets that retain information about a particular GNN prediction\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer}. PGExplainer takes a different route: it learns a parameterized generator so that explanations can be produced collectively for multiple instances\cite{luo2020pgexplainer}. Both methods concern model outputs, but the per-instance optimization and the shared explanation generator have different computational and generalization assumptions. The present pilot uses a single optimized edge mask per selected node; it is inspired by GNNExplainer and is neither a reproduction of that method nor an implementation of PGExplainer.

Evaluation must also separate the model's behavior from the phenomenon a user hopes to understand. GraphFramEx organizes explanation evaluation around user needs and distinguishes necessary explanations, whose removal affects a prediction, from sufficient explanations, whose retained content preserves it\cite{amara2022graphframex}. The deletion control used here asks only a necessity-like question for the model's current predicted class: does removal of the highest-ranked edge set reduce that class logit more than an equal-budget random deletion? It does not test whether the selected edges alone are sufficient, and it does not compare the mask with annotated anomaly mechanisms. Broader analyses of GNN explainers similarly examine faithfulness, stability, and other properties rather than treating one perturbation score as a universal quality measure\cite{agarwal2022probing}.

Finally, deletion is not a neutral operation on a graph. Removing edges can alter message-passing pathways and can move the input away from the distribution on which the model was fitted; a fidelity score can therefore depend on the construction of the perturbed graph and its reference distribution\cite{zheng2024robust}. Our equal-size random control estimates a useful within-node contrast, but both deletion conditions preserve the original degree-normalization coefficients. This makes the comparison reproducible while leaving sensitivity to re-normalization and distribution shift unresolved. The distinction matters when interpreting the signed differences reported below.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Data and Splits}
We used the DGL Reddit graph distributed by the official GADBench repository\cite{gadbenchrepo}. It contains 10,984 nodes, 64-dimensional node features, 366 anomalous-node labels (3.33\% of nodes), and 168,016 raw directed edge records. The source labels and graph construction were not altered. Before modeling, the experiment script checks the SHA-256 digest of the uncompressed source file: \texttt{\scriptsize 575caca4\allowbreak 2abdb659\allowbreak cda70017\allowbreak 4854f7c1\allowbreak 542d0cb7\allowbreak f79f6766\allowbreak 12d4a838\allowbreak 5142a0fa}. The compressed input sent to the controlled runner has a separate digest, \texttt{\scriptsize ce1ad69e\allowbreak a915d085\allowbreak 7495b7cc\allowbreak 75dc8853\allowbreak 5ac014b3\allowbreak 198f8213\allowbreak c7c35b09\allowbreak 347190d6}; these values identify different file layers.

For message passing, we converted the source adjacency to an undirected binary matrix by taking the union with its transpose, added self-loops, and used symmetric degree normalization. The three official train, validation, and test masks were checked for overlap and applied as supplied. Each mask-specific run fitted feature standardization on training nodes only; training labels were used for model fitting, validation labels for early stopping or hyperparameter selection, and test labels only for final evaluation. We call these mask-specific runs split-level trials 0--2 and assigned seeds 17--19 in order. All three trials reuse one graph, so their dispersion describes sensitivity to the official masks and seeds, not variability across independent graphs.

The GCN follows a transductive protocol. During training, the full graph structure and the feature vectors of test nodes participate in message passing, while test labels remain excluded from optimization and model selection. This setting is appropriate to the supplied fixed-graph masks but differs from predicting nodes on an unseen graph or under an inductive split. We retain this distinction throughout the interpretation.

\subsection{Detection Models}
For graph $G=(V,E)$ with feature matrix $X$, we trained a two-layer GCN with hidden width 32. Let $A$ denote the symmetrized, binarized adjacency after adding self-loops and let $D$ be its degree matrix. The normalized adjacency is $\tilde A=D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$. The forward pass is
\begin{equation}
  H=\operatorname{ReLU}(\tilde A X W_0),\qquad Z=\tilde A H W_1+b_1,
\end{equation}
where $W_0$ and $W_1$ are learned weight matrices and $b_1$ is the output bias. The model outputs two class logits per node; the anomaly score is the softmax probability of class 1. We minimized class-weighted cross-entropy on training nodes, with normal-class weight 1 and anomaly-class weight equal to the training-set negative-to-positive ratio. Adam used learning rate $0.01$ and weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$. Training ran for at most 60 epochs. After each epoch, validation AUPRC was calculated; the checkpoint with the highest validation AUPRC was retained, and optimization stopped after ten consecutive epochs without an improvement greater than $10^{-5}$. The seed-specific best epochs and stopping epochs are reported in Appendix~A.

The comparison model was logistic regression using node features only. For each split, the standard scaler was fitted to training features, and logistic regression used balanced class weights with at most 500 solver iterations. We selected $C$ from $\{0.1,1,10\}$ by validation AUPRC and retained the corresponding test scores. Thus both models used the same official masks, and test labels did not inform checkpoint or hyperparameter selection. This baseline tests whether the GCN improves on a linear feature-only rule; it does not isolate the causal contribution of topology from all other architectural differences.

We report average precision (AUPRC) as the primary ranking metric because anomalies are rare, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) as a complementary pairwise-ranking measure. Metrics were computed on each test mask without selecting a binary decision threshold. We report the arithmetic mean and sample standard deviation across the three split-level trials. Since those trials share the same graph and there are only three of them, the summary is descriptive; we do not treat its standard deviation as an independent-sample confidence interval and perform no significance test.

\subsection{Local Edge Masks and Deletion Controls}
The explanation pilot used the split-0 GCN and a fixed, label-stratified sample of up to six anomalous and six normal test nodes. Labels were used only to form this balanced pilot sample, not in mask optimization. Sampling used random seed 2026; each node's mask and random-deletion controls used seed $1000+v$, where $v$ is the node identifier. This design gives both label groups representation but is not a prevalence-representative sample and was not intended to estimate population-level explanation quality.

For each selected target node $v$, we extracted its two-hop induced subgraph. Candidate mask variables were assigned to non-self-loop undirected edge pairs in that subgraph that had at least one endpoint in the target's one-hop neighborhood; each parameter was shared by the two directed entries for the same pair. All other local edges and all node features remained fixed. If $m_v$ denotes the number of candidate pairs and $M_e=\sigma(\omega_e)$ the mask probability for pair $e$, the optimization objective was
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mask}}(\omega)=-\log p_{\theta}(c_v\mid G_v(M),X_v)
  +0.05\,\frac{1}{m_v}\sum_e M_e
  +0.01\,\frac{1}{m_v}\sum_e h(M_e),
\end{equation}
where $c_v$ is the class predicted for $v$ on the unmasked local graph, $p_{\theta}$ is the fitted GCN's softmax output, and $h(q)=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q)$ is Bernoulli entropy. Mask logits were initialized to zero and optimized for 35 steps with Adam at learning rate 0.15. The first term preserves the model's original predicted class, while the two regularizers favor a sparse, low-entropy mask. This lightweight implementation does not include the original GNNExplainer feature-mask objective or all details of its mutual-information formulation.

We ranked candidate pairs by learned mask logit and set $k_v=\min(10,\max(1,\operatorname{round}(0.1m_v)))$. For each target, one perturbation deleted the top-$k_v$ pairs; 20 controls independently sampled the same number of candidate pairs without replacement within each control. Deleting an undirected pair removed both directed entries. The local graph retained the full graph's original degree-normalization coefficients in every perturbation, so degrees were not recomputed after edge removal. Targets were skipped if fewer than three candidate pairs were available, the local graph exceeded 3,000 nodes, or the candidate count exceeded 20,000.

For a target with original predicted class $c_v$, let $z_{v,c_v}(G_v)$ be its logit on the unmasked local graph and $z_{v,c_v}(G_v\setminus S)$ its logit after deleting edge set $S$. We measured
\begin{equation}
  d^{\mathrm{mask}}_v=z_{v,c_v}(G_v)-z_{v,c_v}(G_v\setminus S^{\mathrm{mask}}_v),\qquad
  d^{\mathrm{rand}}_{vj}=z_{v,c_v}(G_v)-z_{v,c_v}(G_v\setminus S^{\mathrm{rand}}_{vj}),
\end{equation}
and defined the paired difference
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_v=d^{\mathrm{mask}}_v-\frac{1}{20}\sum_{j=1}^{20}d^{\mathrm{rand}}_{vj}.
\end{equation}
Positive $\Delta_v$ means that deleting the mask-ranked set reduced the current predicted-class logit more than the mean equal-size random deletion did. A negative value means that the random deletions produced the larger mean reduction. The quantity is expressed in logit units and summarizes one target, one fitted model, and one perturbation protocol. It does not measure probability change, explanation sufficiency, semantic correctness, or causal effect on the labeled phenomenon.

\subsection{Local Explanation Perturbations}
Explanation optimization succeeded for 9 of the 12 sampled nodes; the three skipped cases all exceeded the 3,000-node local-subgraph limit. Four successful cases had anomaly labels and five had normal labels. The model predicted the anomaly class for two of the four labeled anomalies and for one of the five labeled normal nodes. Thus six of the nine sampled labels agreed with the model's predicted class, but this label-stratified pilot is too small and deliberately non-representative to estimate classification accuracy. The full node-level outcomes are listed in Appendix~B.

Across the nine successful explanations, mean $\Delta_v$ was $0.4585$ (sample SD, $0.6891$) and the median was $0.0025$. The mean was $-0.0015$ among anomaly-labeled nodes ($n=4$) and $0.8264$ among normal-labeled nodes ($n=5$). All three values near $1.377$ came from normal-labeled nodes that the model also predicted as normal; the remaining six node-level differences were within $0.016$ of zero. Among the four explained anomaly-labeled nodes, the largest positive $\Delta_v$ was $0.0069$. Appendix~C reports each mask-ranked logit drop and its 20-control random-deletion summary.

\section{Discussion}
The detector comparison does not show a stable advantage for neighborhood aggregation over the feature-only baseline. The GCN had the higher test AUPRC on two masks and the lower value on one; the mean gap was small relative to the observed split-wise spread. This pattern is compatible with the broader motivation of GADBench, where simple neighborhood-aware tree ensembles can compete with specialized GNNs, but our experiment did not include those stronger baseline families\cite{tang2023gadbench}. It therefore cannot answer whether a GCN is preferable to the best available supervised detector. Nor is the GCN-versus-logistic comparison a controlled topology ablation: the models differ in both their function class and their access to edges.

The explanation pilot's median difference was close to zero, while three large positive values pulled the mean upward. The node-level records show that those values occurred for normal-labeled nodes whose predicted class was also normal. Conversely, two of the four explained anomaly-labeled nodes were predicted as normal and had near-zero deletion differences. These cases concern two different questions: whether the model's output depends on a selected edge set, and whether the model's output captures the labeled anomaly phenomenon. A mask can be sensitive for a correct normal prediction without identifying an anomaly mechanism; a mask on a mistaken anomaly prediction explains model behavior rather than the missed phenomenon. GraphFramEx makes this target distinction explicit in its evaluation framework\cite{amara2022graphframex}.

The measured deletion contrast is also narrower than a complete explanation-fidelity assessment. It is a necessity-like test on the model's predicted class, using an edge mask optimized to preserve that same class. It does not test sufficiency by retaining only the selected edges, compare edge rankings against trusted labels, or measure stability across explainer initializations. Its 20 random controls provide a within-node reference distribution, but the reported sample standard deviation describes only those random edge sets. Graph-explanation evaluations have shown that fidelity can depend on perturbation construction and reference distribution\cite{agarwal2022probing,zheng2024robust}; here, fixed degree normalization leaves the behavior under re-normalized graphs unknown.

Two follow-up tests would resolve the most consequential uncertainties. First, an evaluation across several benchmark graphs should compare the GCN with neighborhood-aggregation tree ensembles and repeat explanation optimization across seeds, reporting results separately for correct and incorrect predictions. This would reveal whether the present split and node heterogeneity recurs beyond one graph. Second, controlled graphs with planted anomalous motifs and known explanatory edges could test whether an explanation recovers the phenomenon, while matched perturbations and re-normalized adjacency test whether the mask's apparent necessity is robust to the deletion protocol. These tests would distinguish model sensitivity from semantic explanation quality, which the current labels and run cannot do.

\section{Limitations}
Several limitations bound these conclusions. The detector study uses one graph, one supervised GCN, and a feature-only logistic-regression comparator; it does not include the strongest neighborhood-aggregation or tree-ensemble baselines from the wider benchmark. The three official masks reuse the same graph and are not independent replications, so their standard deviation cannot represent cross-graph uncertainty. The transductive protocol also exposes test-node features and topology to message passing, even though test labels are withheld.

The explanation study covers 12 selected candidates and yields nine masks, all from split 0. Two anomaly-labeled nodes and one normal-labeled node were excluded by the size limit. Each explanation uses one mask initialization and a fixed top-10 deletion budget; no repeated-seed stability estimate or sufficiency test was produced. The dataset has no edge-level explanation ground truth. As a result, the perturbation score measures local sensitivity of this GCN to one specified intervention, not the correctness of the edge mask as an explanation of anomalous behavior. Finally, retaining the original degree-normalization coefficients after deletion and the possibility of out-of-distribution perturbed inputs may change the measured sensitivity. The source dataset's label-generation process and licensing terms were not independently audited in this study.

\section{Conclusion}
Across the three official GADBench Reddit splits, the two-layer GCN and feature-only logistic regression achieved similar detection metrics, with the leading model varying by split. In a nine-node explanation pilot, most mask-versus-random deletion differences were close to zero and three large effects came from predicted-normal nodes. This study therefore supports a narrow methodological point: detector scores, model-prediction sensitivity, and explanations of the anomaly phenomenon require separate evidence. The recorded run does not establish consistent GCN superiority or that local edge masks recover real anomaly causes. Resolving those questions will require stronger supervised baselines, multiple independent graphs, repeated explanation runs, and datasets with trusted edge-level ground truth.
