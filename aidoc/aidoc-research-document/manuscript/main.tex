\begin{abstract}
This brief is a pilot ablation on evidence grounding, not a study of the original retrieval-augmented-generation question. All empirical claims in it are unverified pending a confirmed experiment-run Evidence entry. We describe a four-arm ablation with eight generations per arm on a single research question, in which the arms vary whether Evidence is present in the stage input and how strongly citation is required, together with a citation instruction, schema enforcement, and a repair retry. The reported pilot signals --- coverage of 1.00 and no fabricated ids with Evidence present, roughly tenfold higher declared uncertainty under the citation instruction, minimal schema-enforcement effect with no repair trigger, and, without Evidence, all-fabricated ids, seven-of-eight enforcement rejections, and zero repairs --- remain unverified single-question observations. None of the three human-confirmed background papers directly addresses retrieval-augmented-generation provenance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Retrieval-augmented generation pipelines can emit citations that do not correspond to any retrieved source. Two broad remedies are available. The first is a prompt-level citation instruction: the model is asked to cite its Evidence. The second is code-level enforcement: a schema check rejects generations whose cited identifiers do not resolve, optionally followed by a repair retry. This brief motivates a pilot ablation that contrasts the two mechanisms as ways to suppress fabricated citations. The pilot is deliberately narrow, and the contrast between declared honesty and accepted output is the object of study rather than a completed finding.

The abstract of the distributed attribute representation paper, \path{paper-b2b2f01594862b01e12f}, proposes a third-order multiplicative model in which word context and attribute vectors interact to predict the next word, and reports tasks including sentiment classification, cross-lingual document classification, and blog authorship attribution; this is background by analogy only and does not address retrieval-augmented-generation provenance (\path{claim.attribute-representation-background}, confidence 0.85).

\section{Method}
The study is a four-arm ablation with eight generations per arm on a single research question. The arms vary whether Evidence is present in the stage input and how strongly citation is required. In the Evidence-present condition the stage input contains the evidence items; in the no-Evidence condition it does not. The citation instruction asks the model to cite Evidence. Schema enforcement checks the generated structure and rejects a generation that fails the schema. A repair retry re-invokes the model after a rejection. The sequence flow of the pipeline under study is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:sequence-flow}, and its module graph is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:module-graph}.

The paired, intervention-style logic of this design is only analogically motivated by the human-confirmed review abstract \path{paper-50abf243f9f4e962403b}, which reports that stronger benchmarks incorporate interventional or counterfactual reasoning. The actual four-arm design is not recorded in any confirmed Evidence entry, and transposing that rationale from causal-reasoning benchmarks to claim-level provenance is an unvalidated analogy (\path{claim.ablation-design}, confidence 0.4).

\section{Results}
With Evidence present in the stage input, claim--evidence coverage was 1.00 in every arm and no cited id was fabricated, indicating that adding the citation requirement imposed no measurable citation cost in that condition (\path{claim.coverage-with-evidence}, confidence 0.25). Instructing the model to cite Evidence raised declared uncertainty roughly tenfold relative to the condition without that instruction (\path{claim.instruction-raises-declared-uncertainty}, confidence 0.25). Schema enforcement added almost nothing beyond the prompt instruction and never triggered a repair in the Evidence-present arms (\path{claim.schema-enforcement-minimal}, confidence 0.25). With no Evidence in the stage input, every cited id was fabricated, enforcement rejected seven of eight generations, and the repair retry never repaired one (\path{claim.no-evidence-fabrication}, confidence 0.25). The no-evidence arm is the only arm in which fabrication was possible by construction, so this rate is not comparable across arms. The repair retry could not repair a model that had nothing to cite, repairing zero of the rejected no-evidence generations (\path{claim.retry-repaired-none}, confidence 0.25).

Each of these is a single-question pilot observation. The experiment-run Evidence entry that would confirm them is pending/unverified and was therefore not used as claim evidence. Figure~\ref{fig:experiment-results} presents the pilot results.

\section{Analysis}
Prompt instruction changes declared honesty, whereas code enforcement is what stops a fabricated citation from being accepted, and the retry cannot repair a model that has nothing to cite (\path{claim.instruction-vs-enforcement}, confidence 0.2). This interpretation rests on an eight-generations-per-arm, single-question pilot and is not backed by any confirmed Evidence entry, so it must not be reported as a verified result.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The sample is eight generations per arm on a single research question, so the observed effects are a pilot signal rather than a population estimate; no uncertainty interval, variance summary, or significance test is available.
\item Only the no-evidence arm could fabricate citations by construction, so fabrication rates are not comparable across arms and the seven-of-eight rejection figure applies only to that arm.
\item The experiment-run Evidence entry \path{evidence-19e177ec-9a6c-4bac-b897-03c7a6f61b50} is recorded in the stage input ledger with verificationStatus pending and does not resolve as confirmed Evidence in this project, so no empirical claim in this brief cites it or is treated as verified.
\item No retrieval-augmented-generation paper is present in the confirmed Evidence set; the three human-confirmed paper entries concern causal-reasoning benchmarks, cyber threat attribution, and distributed attribute representations, so they are background only and do not directly support evidence-grounding mechanisms.
\item Paper 2407.08029v1 is a preprint whose venue, venueLevel, peerReviewed, citationCount, and hasCode fields are unset, and its benchmark criteria are described only at abstract level, so any derived checklist is partly extrapolated.
\item The experiment is recorded as planned with a human-approved plan only; no structured Experiment Run has been executed and no unsupported-claim-rate result exists.
\item No dataset identity, size, version, license, code artifact, environment specification, or deterministic seed was supplied, so reproducibility cannot be assessed or claimed.
\item Ground-truth supported-by-cited-span labels and annotator agreement are unavailable, and the enforcement and repair behavior is observed on a single model and prompt scaffold, so generalization is unestablished.
\end{itemize}
