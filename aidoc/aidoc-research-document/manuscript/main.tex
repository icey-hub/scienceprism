\begin{abstract}
This study compares three prompting conditions---direct, chain-of-thought, and an explicit answer format---for a reasoning model over 200 GSM8K test questions at temperature 0, for 600 generations in total. Every empirical number reported here rests on an experiment record that is not yet confirmed in this project: the declared accuracies, the per-item pairing counts, the hidden-trace disagreement counts, and the grading discrepancy are unverified pending human confirmation. The work is therefore presented as a proposal and as an interpretation of an unconfirmed record, not as a confirmed result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The question this study frames is whether the prompting condition changes outcomes for a model that already reasons in a hidden channel. For conventional language models, generating a chain of intermediate reasoning steps substantially improves complex reasoning \path{paper-wei-2022-cot}, and a single zero-shot trigger phrase can elicit such reasoning without exemplars \path{paper-kojima-2022-zero-shot}. A reasoning model such as DeepSeek-R1, by contrast, is trained to produce long reasoning traces through reinforcement learning, so reasoning already occurs in a hidden channel \path{paper-deepseek-2025-r1}. If that hidden channel is already active, an explicit chain-of-thought instruction may add little. The background this result speaks to is therefore the chain-of-thought literature, the zero-shot reasoning literature, self-consistency decoding, the GSM8K benchmark, and work on reasoning models.

\section{Related Work}
Cobbe et al. introduce GSM8K as a dataset of 8.5K grade-school math word problems on which even the largest transformer models fail to reach high test performance \path{paper-cobbe-2021-gsm8k}. Wei et al. report that generating a chain of intermediate reasoning steps substantially improves complex reasoning \path{paper-wei-2022-cot}. Kojima et al. show that a single zero-shot trigger phrase elicits chain-of-thought reasoning without exemplars, which motivates varying only the instruction rather than adding demonstrations (claim \path{claim-zeroshot-background}; evidence \path{paper-kojima-2022-zero-shot}; confidence 0.8). Wang et al. propose self-consistency as a decoding strategy that replaces greedy decoding in chain-of-thought prompting and improves reasoning accuracy, making it a candidate next lever if prompt-level changes prove inert (claim \path{claim-selfconsistency-background}; evidence \path{paper-wang-2022-self-consistency}; confidence 0.8). DeepSeek-R1 is trained to produce long reasoning traces through reinforcement learning, so reasoning already occurs in a hidden channel \path{paper-deepseek-2025-r1}.

\section{Analysis}
Read against the background that chain-of-thought prompting helps conventional LLMs (Wei et al.) while a reasoning model such as DeepSeek-R1 already reasons in a hidden channel, the unverified experiment record suggests that for a reasoning model the prompting condition barely changes the outcome and that the dominant measured error sources are item difficulty and the grading rule rather than the prompt. This conclusion is an interpretation of an unverified record, not a confirmed result (claim \path{claim-conclusion}; evidence \path{paper-deepseek-2025-r1}, \path{paper-wei-2022-cot}; confidence 0.25).

A plausible mechanism, not demonstrated in this project, is that because a reasoning model is trained to produce hidden reasoning traces, an explicit chain-of-thought instruction adds little. Kojima et al. show a single zero-shot trigger phrase elicits reasoning without exemplars, and Wang et al. propose self-consistency decoding as a strategy that improves reasoning accuracy, making it a candidate next lever; neither the mechanism nor the prompting result is confirmed here (claim \path{claim-mechanism}; evidence \path{paper-deepseek-2025-r1}, \path{paper-kojima-2022-zero-shot}, \path{paper-wang-2022-self-consistency}; confidence 0.2).

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item One model (\path{global:deepseek-v4.1-flash}), one dataset (GSM8K test split), one temperature (0), and one generation per item, so the results characterise that configuration and do not estimate a population.
\item Every empirical number depends on an experiment record (\path{evidence-cot-gsm8k-experiment}) that is not present as confirmed Evidence in this project's evidence graph; those numbers remain unverified pending human confirmation.
\item No confidence intervals, repeated sampling, or per-item difficulty model are available, so no uncertainty estimate accompanies the reported accuracy figures.
\item Exact-match and numeric-equivalence grading were applied post hoc, and the 2 parse failures are not broken down by condition.
\item The background papers address conventional prompting, datasets, and reasoning models, not retrieval-augmented-generation provenance, so they can frame but cannot confirm any claim about claim-to-source traceability.
\item The study design, the direction, and the conclusion are the human's approved framing; the assistant has not independently verified the run or the experiment command.
\item No dataset, code, or run artifacts were supplied with the stage input, so data and code availability cannot be asserted.
\end{itemize}
